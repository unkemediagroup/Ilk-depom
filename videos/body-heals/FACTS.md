# Facts used in the film

General-interest facts, simplified; no medical advice and no diagnosis.

| Claim in the film | Source | Wording / note |
|---|---|---|
| Within seconds of a cut, platelets rush in to plug the leak. | A clinical review of wound healing — https://www.dentistryiq.com/articles/2012/06/a-clinical-review-of-wound-healing.html ; PMC review https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6556139 | "Hemostasis is the initial phase that occurs within seconds to minutes after the initial insult"; the clot is "platelets embedded in a mesh of cross-linked fibrin fibers". |
| Over the next few days, new skin cells crawl across the gap until it closes. | Same clinical review | Epithelial cells migrate across the wound "0.5 mm to 1.0 mm per day", peaking by about day seven (small cuts). |
| A broken bone builds a bridge (callus) across the break, then turns it back into solid bone; many breaks heal in about six to eight weeks. | NHS 111 Wales, broken collarbone — https://111.wales.nhs.uk/brokencollarbone ; RUH Bath wrist leaflet — https://www.ruh.nhs.uk/patients/patient_information/ORT040_Advice_after_breaking_your_wrist.pdf | "most people recover … in 6 to 8 weeks"; wrist "in the plaster cast or splint for 6 to 8 weeks". Varies by bone (femur, tibia take longer), hence "many breaks". |
| Surgeons can remove up to two-thirds of a healthy liver, and it grows back. | PMC review of liver regeneration — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3377328 ; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3272806 | Two-thirds partial hepatectomy is the classic model; regeneration stops when the original liver-to-body mass ratio is restored. Regeneration is weaker in a chronically damaged liver, hence "healthy". |
