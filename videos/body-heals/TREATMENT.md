# Did You Know Your Body Can Heal Itself? — Treatment

Style: Watercolor Brush · 1080×1920 vertical (YouTube Shorts) · 30 fps · ~50 s · English voice + burned English subtitles + `.srt`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A field journal of repairs (chosen).** One tall journal page, "Notes on a body that mends". The camera tilts down the sheet from one specimen to the next: a cut finger, a broken bone, a liver. Each one is painted *damaged*, then repaints itself whole. Ends by pulling back to the whole page, then pushing back to the finger: the cut is now a faint pale line.
2. **A countdown of healing speeds.** Seconds (a clot) → days (skin) → weeks (bone) → months (liver). Clear, but a list with no turn; the "months" end is flat.
3. **Before / after split.** Each organ shown twice, side by side. Reads at thumbnail size but kills the medium's best move (repainting over time on one spot).

Why 1: watercolour's native move is *painted transformation* — a wash that heals over a wound is literally what the topic is. A tall sheet suits 9:16 and gives a vertical tilt as the journey. The opening is a hand-written question answered by painting; the ending echoes the opening cut.

## 2. Logline and arc

A red brush stroke cuts across a clean journal page, and the page repairs it — then shows that bone and liver do the same.

- **Setup (0–4 s):** the question is written on blank paper; a red stroke slashes across a painted fingertip.
- **Development (4–26 s):** clot → skin closes; tilt down → bone cracks → bridge → solid.
- **Turn (26–38 s):** a breath of silence; "Your liver can do something even stranger." Two-thirds of the liver washes away in mist… and paints itself back.
- **Ending (38–50 s):** pull back: the three specimens are one body on the page. Push back to the fingertip: only a faint line. Title written by hand: "Your body heals itself."

## 3. Benchmark

- **Naturalist field-journal plates** (specimen + hand note + leader line) — learn: one hero object per view, generous margins, notes that measure.
- **Medical-illustration process plates** (stage 1 → stage 2 → stage 3 of a healing wound) — learn: each stage legible as a silhouette; colour carries the stage (red = injury, warm pink = new tissue, ivory = bone).
- Don't take: any specific plate, anatomy drawing, typography or layout.

## 6. Cue map

- 80 bpm, 4/4 (bar = 3.0 s). Key D major, open voicings.
- Bars 1–2 (0–6): kalimba (pluck) motif, alone. Red slash on beat 2 of bar 2.
- Bars 3–5 (6–15): + harp arpeggios in 8ths, soft upright piano chords on bar starts.
- Bars 6–8 (15–24): + flute line (long notes), bass on roots (upright piano low, no notes below MIDI 48 in pads).
- 24–26: thin out → 26.0–27.2 silence.
- 27.2: one glockenspiel note (accent) as the first sound after silence; kalimba returns alone.
- 30–37 (regrow): harp + piano rebuild bar by bar (music "regrows" with the liver), a glockenspiel tick on each regrown lobe.
- 37.5–50: full but gentle: flute melody restates the opening kalimba motif; ends on an open D chord, ringing out under the title; last glockenspiel when the brush lifts.

## 8. Subtitle and title design

- Subtitles on the paper (no box): centred, serif italic (Cormorant Garamond Italic) ~58 px at 1080 width, max 2 balanced lines, ink at 78 % over a paper-coloured halo, in the bottom 18 % of the frame (kept clear of the subject and of the Shorts UI).
- Hand notes in Caveat with pen leader lines; measured labels in IBM Plex Mono, wide tracking.
- Title "Your body heals itself." written by hand in Caveat, large, in the ink colour, with the vermilion accent only on the healed line.
- Numbers spelled out for the TTS, digits on screen.

## 9. Facts (no medical advice; see FACTS.md)

Platelets plug a wound within seconds to minutes; new skin closes a small cut over days; many fractures heal in about 6–8 weeks; surgeons can remove up to about two-thirds of a healthy liver and it regrows.
