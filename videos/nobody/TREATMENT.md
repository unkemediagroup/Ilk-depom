# Nobody Is Coming to Save You — Treatment

Style: Woodcut Print · 1080×1920 (YouTube Shorts) · 24 fps · 29.6 s (the user's own voice, ElevenLabs "Adam") · English captions in the paper margin.
User rules: no carving/scratch foley at all (clean sound: voice + music + a few low impacts); energetic, not dull.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One figure, one block, recut (chosen).** The same lone figure through eight plates: waiting under stars → tiny in the dark → the fire lights in the chest → takes up the knife → carves a road up a mountain → summit, light → lifts the stone → "GO GET IT" pulled as a print.
2. **A wordless-novel list.** Excuses as eight separate vignettes, each crossed out. Reads like a slideshow; no single arc.
3. **Two blocks in turn.** A rescuer who never arrives alternating with the figure who works. Clever, but splits a 30 s film in two.

Why 1: the voice is a single arc from fear to action; one figure doing it gives the viewer someone to become. Carving = time is literally "the heavy lifting".

## 2. Logline and arc

A figure waiting in the dark for rescue realises none is coming, takes up the knife and carves its own road to the summit.
Setup (0–10 s): waiting, stars going out, alone. Turn (10.4 s): "let it set you free" — the one colour, a burnt orange, lights in the chest. Ending (27.7 s): "Now go get it!" — a mirrored carved title becomes a true print.

## 3. Benchmark

Wordless woodcut novels (plates that each hold one moment, figure as black silhouette with white halo) and modern sports-brand anthem spots (cut on the beat, one image per idea, build to a single shout). Learn: one idea per plate, pace accelerates, hold on the peak. Don't take: any plate, figure design, logo, slogan or music.

## 5. Beat sheet / cue map

Music in D minor, 96 bpm (beat 0.625 s). 0–10: contrabass + cellos drone, sparse frame drum. 10.4: lift (open fifth, cellos rise). 14.3–18: pizzicato pulse. 18–22.2: drive — spiccato 8ths, a gran cassa hit on each "stop" (18.48, 20.96, 22.44). 22.4–25.6: full: timpani roll into the summit, tam-tam at 23.9 ("hero"). 27.1–27.7: near silence. 27.75: biggest hit on "Now" + final chord; ring out to the end.
